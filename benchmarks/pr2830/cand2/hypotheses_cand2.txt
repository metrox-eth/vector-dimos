 S4. THE MONEY IS IN A GLOBAL ORDER, NOT IN ROOM SEGMENTATION. Memo header point
     4 and section (iii): P2 Explore, Table II, single robot, metric in metres:
     a global TSP ordering of frontiers buys about 31 % of travel on large
     scenes, and adding room-awareness on top buys only about 4.9 % more.
     -> CANDIDATE T.

1. THE TWO CANDIDATES, EXACTLY AS THEY WILL RUN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Both are HARNESS-SIDE wrappers in the style of fix_hysteresis.py, installed on
one selector INSTANCE by fix_hysteresis.install(). The two vendored upstream
files stay byte-identical: pr2830/selector_base.py md5
e77c328643c959a49077115e8a341f2c and pr2830/selector_head.py md5
1ccc0c69fe88a72e402565feca988d26, re-checked and stated at the end of the
report. Neither wrapper invents a candidate; both only re-order the list the
selector's own detect_frontiers / _rank_frontiers produced.

CANDIDATE M, signed direction penalty. fix_momentum.py.
  Precedent: GBPlanner, via the memo (sections (iii) and (v).4), which reads it
  out of gbplanner/src/rrg.cpp:657-676 and config/smb/gbplanner_config.yaml:
  gain * exp(-0.07 * path_length) * exp(-0.3 * direction_deviation).
  Form, applied to the arm's own score:

      adjusted = base_score * exp(-0.07 * route_m) * exp(-ratio * 0.07 * dev)

      dev   = (1 - cos(theta)) / 2, in [0, 1]: 0 straight on, 0.5 across,
              1.0 a full U-turn. Strictly decreasing in the dot product over the
              whole range, which is the SIGNED reading; dimOS's max(0, dot) is
              flat over the whole backwards half.
      theta = angle between the robot's current direction of travel and the
              straight-line bearing to the candidate.
      heading = exponential moving average of the REAL DISPLACEMENT between
              decision poses, alpha = 0.3, refreshed only once the robot is more
              than 0.75 m from the pose of the last refresh (GBPlanner's recipe,
              position direction and not body heading). Before the first refresh
              there is no heading and the direction factor is 1.0.
      route_m = route length robot -> candidate under the PLANNER's own cost
              rule, one Dijkstra wave per decision, the same
              fix_hysteresis.geodesic_field the earlier candidate used.
      ratio = the direction-to-distance weight ratio, SWEPT in {1, 2, 4.3}.
              4.3 is GBPlanner's own 0.3 / 0.07.
  Candidates whose score is not finite and positive, or whose route is infinite,
  keep their original relative order at the END of the list.
  NAMED CONTROL, spec M0: ratio = 0, the distance penalty alone. It is NOT a
  candidate and cannot win the sweep. It exists so the report can say whether
  anything M does is attributable to the direction term at all, because the
  GBPlanner form necessarily carries a distance term too.

CANDIDATE T, lazy TSP ordering. fix_tsp.py. Spec T30.
  Precedent: P2 Explore for the claim, FUEL and TARE for the mechanism, all via
  the memo, section (iii) form C.
  1. one Dijkstra wave from the robot per REPLAN gives the route length to every
     current frontier cluster centroid;
  2. a tour is built over those centroids: nearest-neighbour from the robot, then
     2-opt to convergence, capped at 20 sweeps;
  3. the policy COMMITS: at every decision the next un-consumed tour stop is
     moved to the head of the selector's own list. No per-decision
     re-optimisation;
  4. the tour is re-planned ONLY on one of these, all declared here:
       (a) the next tour stop is no longer among the current candidates, matched
           within 1.0 m - it vanished without being visited;
       (b) the frontier set has churned by more than the DECLARED FRACTION
           0.30, churn being the symmetric difference over the union of the
           centroid set at the last replan and the current one, same 1.0 m
           matching;
       (c) SAFETY VALVE, not part of the precedent and counted separately: the
           same stop has been put at the head twice in a row, so a stop the drive
           planner cannot reach cannot lock the tour forever;
       (d) the tour is exhausted.
     A stop that was ISSUED and has since stopped being a frontier is ADVANCED
     PAST, not re-planned: that is the tour being worked through, and it is the
     whole point of the candidate.
  DECLARED APPROXIMATION: with one wave per replan only the robot-to-centroid
  edges are geodesic; the centroid-to-centroid edges are straight line. FUEL pays
  for the full pairwise A* matrix and calls LKH. Ours is a cheap tour and the
  report will not call it a TSP solver.

3. METRICS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Every headline count is RE-DERIVED FROM THE RAW GOAL COORDINATES of each run,
with that map's own swing threshold, independently of the summarise() fields the
bench wrote, and the number of disagreements is stated (the zero-disagreement
check). analyse_cand2.py, unchanged from the fleet's analyse_fleet.py except for
the per-config budget check declared below.

  swing threshold, per map   half the bounding-box diagonal of that map's
                             body-passable floor, as the fleet bench defined it.
  raw swings                 consecutive issued goals further apart than the
                             threshold.
  REAL crossings  (PRIMARY)  raw minus class N, where class N is a swing whose
                             newly chosen goal was within 6 m of the ROBOT at the
                             moment it was issued: the goal sequence jumped and
                             the robot did not. Straight-line here.
  round trips                a swing followed within 3 further jumps by one whose
                             direction opposes it. Counted on both swing sets;
                             REAL round trips are primary.
  total path, coverage, path to 80 % of the ceiling, goal-to-goal total,
  median robot-to-goal, goals issued, decision time, wall-capped runs: reported.

4. THE HYPOTHESES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Per map, and pooled. A failure on a map is reported as a failure ON THAT MAP and
is never averaged away. A map on which the arm being compared against produces
ZERO real crossings is reported as NO EVENTS TO REMOVE and set aside for H1, H2
and H3 rather than counted as a pass or a fail: the metric cannot discriminate
there. (On the fleet this was hk_allaround, whose threshold is 35.13 m.)

H1  stock+M<best> against stock, on the maps where crossings exist:
      (1) total REAL crossings must drop;
      (2) total REAL round trips must drop. When both arms have none the literal
          clause cannot hold; the weaker reading "must not rise" is printed
          alongside and the difference is flagged, never silently substituted;
      (3) median total path must be within +5 % IN EACH CONFIGURATION TAKEN
          ALONE, not only pooled;
      (4) median final coverage must be within -5 % IN EACH CONFIGURATION TAKEN
          ALONE.
    All four must hold for a PASS on that map.

H2  the same four clauses for stock+T30 against stock.

H3  the same four clauses for pr2830+M<best> against pr2830.

H4  candidate T's own precedent claim, on TOTAL PATH rather than on crossings.
    P2 Explore reports about 31 % of travel saved by a global TSP order on LARGE
    scenes. LARGE IS DECLARED HERE, BEFORE THE RUN, by body-passable floor area
    from the fleet gate table: >= 100 m2. That is hk_allaround (544 m2),
    hk_entrance (229 m2), hk_park (157 m2) and hk_elevator (140 m2). The other
    four - hk_office 69 m2, bigoffice 48 m2, bigoffice_hc 48 m2, go2_short
    34 m2 - are the small maps and H4 does not apply to them.
    H4 holds on a large map when the median total path of stock+T30 is LOWER
    than the median total path of stock on that map. Reported per map and
    pooled over the four. No percentage target is claimed: 31 % is P2 Explore's
    number on P2 Explore's scenes, not a prediction for ours.

OPTIONAL COMBINED ARM. Only if BOTH M and T pass H1/H2 on at least half of the
maps where the metric can discriminate. If that gate fires, one further arm
stock+M<best>+T30 is run, the two wrappers chained left to right so that T has
the last word, on the same grid; if it does not fire, the combined arm is NOT run
and the report says the gate did not fire.

6. WHAT WOULD MAKE THIS JOB WRONG, WRITTEN DOWN NOW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 - One recording per floor, no repetition, no variance estimate. Every number is
   a count or a median over 12 paired starts (6 on the two shipped-only maps).
   Nothing here is a statistical claim.
 - Both candidates cost more per decision than stock. On hk_allaround, whose runs
   are wall-capped, a more expensive policy explores less inside the same 600 s
   of real time. That is a confound on that map and only on that map.
 - Candidate M carries a distance term as well as a direction term, because the
   GBPlanner form does. The control arm M0 is the only thing separating them and
   it runs on bigoffice only.
 - The simulated pose is perfect and unknown is a wall by construction. A
   reversal in simulation is a decision, never noise.
 - The rover is 46 cm and the Go2 that recorded these floors is about 31 cm, so
   the bench explores a skeleton of each floor. Unchanged from the fleet bench.
