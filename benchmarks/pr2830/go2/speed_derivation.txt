==============================================================================
THE GO2 PROFILE, DERIVED FROM dimOS' OWN RECORDINGS
derive_speed.py, 2026-08-29. Nothing here is assumed; every number is measured
on the SAME SQLite stores the maps of this bench were extracted from.
==============================================================================

SOURCE. The `odom` stream of each recording. Where the store's pose_x/pose_y
INDEX columns carry a real trace it is read straight out of SQL; where they
are zeroed or null (5 of 9 recordings) the PoseStamped payload is decoded by
dimOS' own SqliteStore reader, and the frame_id is checked to be `world` -
the extraction REFUSES a store whose odom is not in the world frame rather
than inventing pose maths. Which path each recording took is in the `source`
column below. On go2_bigoffice, where both paths exist, they are the same
trace: the SQL columns ARE the decoded pose.

METHOD, declared before any result was looked at:
  1. poses sorted by ts, duplicate timestamps dropped;
  2. the trace is cut into SEGMENTS wherever two samples are more than 1.0 s
     apart - a recording pause is not a standstill and must not be averaged;
  3. speed is measured over a sliding 0.5 s window of wall time inside a
     segment: straight-line displacement over elapsed time. 0.5 s is long
     enough that odom jitter at ~19 Hz does not dominate and short enough
     that a turn in place is not smeared into a walk;
  4. a window is MOVING at 0.05 m/s or more (0.03 and 0.10 printed beside it);
  5. median and p90 over the moving windows only.
Straight-line displacement UNDER-states speed on a curve, so every speed here
is a lower bound on path speed.

THE CHOICE, AND ITS SENSITIVITY. Two poolings were available and they differ:
  window-weighted pooled median over the five HK recordings : 0.6029 m/s
  median of the five per-map medians                        : 0.9408 m/s
  per-map medians                                           : hk_office=0.428, hk_park=0.941, hk_elevator=0.884, hk_entrance=0.944, hk_allaround=0.945
The window-weighted pooled median is used. It is the smaller of the two, so
it is the choice LEAST favourable to the premise that the shipped 15 s
configuration becomes a usable instrument at Go2 speed, and it treats each
recorded second of walking equally rather than each floor equally. The spread
is real and is a caveat, not a rounding: hk_office, a narrow desk-aisle
office, was walked at 0.43 m/s while hk_allaround was walked at 0.94 m/s.
One speed is used for the whole grid so that the goal-timeout truncation
distance is the same on every map and per-map results stay comparable.

WHAT IT BUYS AT THE UPSTREAM TIMEOUT: 15.0 s x 0.60 m/s = 9.00 m of walk
before the loop pulls the robot off a goal, against 15.0 x 0.15 = 2.25 m for
our rover. That is the whole reason this bench exists.

2. TURN-IN-PLACE RATE  (explore_sim.TURN_RATE)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A window where the body is turning (yaw rate >= 0.10 rad/s) while NOT
translating (window speed < 0.05 m/s) is a turn IN PLACE, which is what
TURN_RATE models. The yaw rate of a walking curve is a different quantity and
is printed beside it so the difference is visible. Yaw comes from the odom
quaternion, unwrapped, over the same 0.5 s windows.

The rover profile turns at 0.5 rad/s. The Go2 pivots about 26 % faster. This
is not cosmetic: turning spends goal-timeout budget, and a 180 deg turn costs
5.0 s at 0.63 rad/s against 6.3 s at 0.5, i.e. 1.3 s more walking inside a
15 s timeout. The pivot sample is small on three of the five floors (12 to 30
windows) because a walking survey rarely stops to rotate; hk_office carries
1 334 of the 1 492 and the pooled median is effectively its median. That is a
caveat, and the direction of the correction is not in doubt.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. LIDAR SPACING  (explore_sim.SCAN_EVERY_M) - measured, and DELIBERATELY
   NOT CHANGED. This is the check that the speed change is not a free lunch.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
explore_sim couples two constants: SPEED_MPS, which fixes how far a goal
timeout truncates a walk, and SCAN_EVERY_M = 0.25 m, one simulated lidar
revolution per 0.25 m of travel - a value calibrated for a 0.15 m/s rover,
about 0.6 revolutions per second. Raising the speed and leaving a per-DISTANCE
scan rate alone would be an optimistic discovery bias IF the real robot could
not deliver a revolution inside every 0.25 m of walk. So it was measured.

METHOD: the `lidar` table's own frame timestamps; the odom trace interpolated
at each of them inside one continuous segment; the distance between two
consecutive frame positions; a pair counts when its implied speed is at least
0.05 m/s, the same standstill threshold as above.

       recorded pace, and 1.7 even at the p90 spacing.

VERDICT: SCAN_EVERY_M STAYS 0.25 m, for both profiles, and the measurement is
what licenses it. The real Go2 lidar streams at 7.7 Hz; at 0.60 m/s that is
about 3.2 revolutions inside every 0.25 m slice, and about
13 at 0.15 m/s. The simulated one-per-0.25 m is the COARSER of
the two at both speeds, so information per metre is limited by geometry, not
by the sensor, at either speed, and keeping the step in distance isolates the
robot profile from the sensor model. The honest way to state the residual: the
sim under-samples the real Go2 lidar by roughly 3x per metre, which makes it
CONSERVATIVE about discovery under the Go2 profile, not optimistic.

==============================================================================
THE PROFILE THIS PRODUCES (go2_profile.py, 'go2')
==============================================================================
  SPEED_MPS          0.15 -> 0.60     derived, section 1
  TURN_RATE          0.50 -> 0.63     derived, section 2
  SCAN_EVERY_M       0.25 -> 0.25     measured and deliberately kept, section 3
  ROBOT_WIDTH_M      0.50 -> 0.35     0.31 m body + the same 4 cm planner margin
  BODY_HALF_WIDTH_M  0.23 -> 0.155    body / 2
  CONTROL_MARGIN_M   0.05 -> 0.05     unchanged, a controller margin
  LETHAL_CLEARANCE_M 0.30 -> 0.225    the same formula: width/2 + margin
  PIVOT_CLEARANCE_M  0.39 -> 0.383    the same rule: half the body diagonal

Body source: Unitree Go2 published standing size 700 x 310 x 400 mm, identical
across Air / Pro / Edu (docs.quadruped.de 'Model Variants | Unitree GO2';
shop.unitree.com/products/unitree-go2). Weight 15 kg. Nominal top speeds are
2.5 to 3.7 m/s depending on variant - four to six times what the surveys were
actually walked at, which is why the recordings and not the datasheet fix the
simulated speed.

NOT TOUCHED by the profile: every dimOS constant. The loop's 0.25 m costmap
inflation, the 15 s goal timeout, the info-gain self-stop and the failed-goal
radius are theirs and stay as shipped.
